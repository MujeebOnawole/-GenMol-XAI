================================================================================
REACTION MECHANISM FOR EXEMPLAR COMPOUNDS - EXPLAINED
================================================================================

This document explains the fragment recombination chemistry used to generate
the three pathogen-selective exemplar compounds from XAI-identified fragments.

================================================================================
GENERAL PRINCIPLE: ACYL TRANSFER REACTIONS
================================================================================

All products are formed via nucleophilic acyl substitution (acyl transfer):

    R-C(=O)-LG  +  H-NR'R"  -->  R-C(=O)-NR'R"  +  LG-H

Where:
    - R-C(=O)-LG = Acid fragment (with leaving group LG)
    - H-NR'R" = Amine fragment (nucleophile)
    - Product = Amide (if R is carbon) or Urea (if R is nitrogen)

REACTION SMIRKS NOTATION:
    [C:1](=[O:2])[O:3].[N:5;H2,H1;!$(N=[!#6])]>>[C:1](=[O:2])[N:5]

This encodes:
    - Atom [C:1] = Carbonyl carbon (preserved)
    - Atom [O:2] = Carbonyl oxygen (preserved)
    - Atom [O:3] = Leaving group oxygen (displaced)
    - Atom [N:5] = Amine nitrogen (attacks carbonyl)

================================================================================
ROUTE CLASSIFICATION
================================================================================

ROUTE A - Native Acids (mildest conditions)
    - Substrates: Carboxylic acids, anhydrides
    - Conditions: Coupling reagents (EDC, HATU), rt to 40°C
    - Mechanism: Activated ester intermediate

ROUTE B - Latent Esters (mild conditions)
    - Substrates: Methyl/ethyl esters
    - Conditions: Base (K2CO3), solvent (MeOH or DMF), 40-60°C
    - Mechanism: Direct aminolysis

ROUTE C - Cyclic Electrophiles (harsh conditions)
    - Substrates: Lactones, oxazolidinones (cyclic carbamates)
    - Conditions: Neat (no solvent), 80-120°C, 12-24h
    - Mechanism: Ring-opening aminolysis

================================================================================
EXEMPLAR COMPOUND 1: CMPD_087 (S. aureus-selective)
================================================================================

TARGET PATHOGEN: Staphylococcus aureus (Gram-positive bacterium)
PREDICTED ACTIVITY: SA=0.982, EC=0.361, CA=0.199
TIER: 3 (Scenario A) - High confidence prediction

FRAGMENTS:
-----------
Acid Fragment: Oxazolidinone-containing substituent
    SMILES: CC(=O)NC[C@H]1CN(c2ccc(-c3ccc(C=N)c(O)c3)c(F)c2)C(=O)O1
    Source: SA_positive_substituents.csv
    Type: Cyclic carbamate (oxazolidinone)
    Attribution: 0.1249 (positive, activity-associated)

Amine Fragment: Dioxolane-quinoline scaffold
    SMILES: CN[C@H]1CO[C@H](CCc2ccnc3ccc(OC)cc23)OC1
    Source: SA_positive_substituents.csv
    Type: Secondary amine (2° amine)
    Attribution: 0.2403 (positive, activity-associated)

REACTION:
---------
Route: C (Cyclic electrophile - oxazolidinone ring-opening)
Conditions: Neat, 100°C, 12-24h
Product Linkage: UREA bond (N-C(=O)-N)

MECHANISM EXPLANATION:
----------------------
1. The oxazolidinone ring contains a cyclic carbamate: -N-C(=O)-O- in a 5-membered ring

2. At elevated temperature (100°C), the secondary amine attacks the carbonyl carbon
   of the oxazolidinone:

       Oxazolidinone-C(=O)-O  +  CH3-NH-R  -->  Oxazolidinone-C(=O)-NH-R  +  -O-

3. The ring opens, releasing the alkoxide which picks up a proton

4. The product contains a UREA linkage: R1-NH-C(=O)-N(CH3)-R2

WHY "NEAT" CONDITIONS:
----------------------
- "Neat" means NO SOLVENT is used
- The amine fragment is used in EXCESS and serves as both:
  (a) The nucleophilic reactant
  (b) The reaction medium (pseudo-solvent)
- Heat (100°C) provides activation energy for ring opening
- No coupling reagents, bases, or catalysts are required
- This is operationally simple and avoids purification of solvents

----------------------

EXEMPLAR COMPOUND 3: CMPD_428 (C. albicans-selective)
================================================================================

TARGET PATHOGEN: Candida albicans (Fungus)
PREDICTED ACTIVITY: SA=0.013, EC=0.044, CA=0.776
TIER: 3 (Scenario A) - High confidence prediction

FRAGMENTS:
-----------
Acid Fragment: Methyl propionate (simple ester)
    SMILES: CCC(=O)OC
    Source: SA_CA_positive_substituents.csv
    Type: Methyl ester (latent acid)
    Attribution: Not recorded (simple fragment)

Amine Fragment: Diphenylamine-pyridinium scaffold
    SMILES: c1ccc(Nc2ccncc2)cc1
    Source: SA_CA_positive_scaffolds.csv
    Type: Secondary amine (2° amine)
    Attribution: Not recorded

REACTION:
---------
Route: B (Latent ester - direct aminolysis)
Conditions: K2CO3, MeOH/DMF, 60°C, 6h
Product Linkage: AMIDE bond (N-C(=O)-C)

MECHANISM EXPLANATION:
----------------------
1. K2CO3 (potassium carbonate) acts as a mild base:
   - Partially deprotonates the amine, increasing nucleophilicity
   - Neutralizes any acid generated during reaction
   - Does NOT hydrolyze the ester (too mild)

2. The amine attacks the ester carbonyl:

       CH3CH2-C(=O)-OCH3  +  Ph-NH-Py  -->  CH3CH2-C(=O)-N(Ph)-Py  +  CH3O-

3. Methoxide leaves and picks up a proton (from K2CO3/solvent)

4. Product is a tertiary AMIDE

WHY SOLVENT IS NEEDED (Route B vs Route C):
-------------------------------------------
- Methyl esters are LESS reactive than oxazolidinones
- The ester lacks ring strain that activates oxazolidinones
- Solvent (MeOH or DMF) is needed to:
  (a) Dissolve both reactants
  (b) Facilitate proton transfer
  (c) Allow lower reaction temperature (60°C vs 100°C)

REAGENT OPTIONS FOR ROUTE B:
----------------------------
Any of the following can be used:
- K2CO3 (potassium carbonate) - mild inorganic base
- MeOH (methanol) - protic solvent, can assist proton transfer
- DMF (dimethylformamide) - polar aprotic solvent, good for amide formation

The figure shows "K2CO3, 60°C" as shorthand for typical conditions.
Full conditions would be: K2CO3, MeOH/DMF (co-solvent), 60°C, 6h

================================================================================
SUMMARY: PRODUCT LINKAGE TYPES
================================================================================

AMIDE BOND (N-C(=O)-C):
-----------------------
- Formed from: Esters, lactones, carboxylic acids, anhydrides
- Acid fragment contributes: R-C(=O)- where R is CARBON
- Found in: CMPD_428 (C. albicans exemplar)
- Prevalence in dataset: 600/700 molecules (85.7%)

UREA BOND (N-C(=O)-N):
----------------------
- Formed from: Oxazolidinones (cyclic carbamates)
- Acid fragment contributes: R-N-C(=O)- where the carbonyl was in the ring
- Found in: CMPD_087 (S. aureus), CMPD_162 (E. coli)
- Prevalence in dataset: 100/700 molecules (14.3%)
- Note: Ureas are MORE polar than amides (additional H-bond donor/acceptor)
